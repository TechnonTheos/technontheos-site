Home Lab Network Audit: Centralized Device Login and a Segmented Firewall

I audited my home lab of enterprise switches, routers and a wireless controller, then prioritized and fixed what I found, starting with management-plane security.

The problem

I run a home lab of enterprise switches, routers and a wireless controller attached to my home network. I audited its documentation, saved configurations and firewall for blind spots, because every device relied on one shared local account and the records had drifted from what the devices actually ran.

Method

I compared documentation and saved configurations against live device output, read the gateway's firewall policy through its read-only API, and replayed two weeks of centralized logs against the alert rules. Every change followed four parts: pre-checks, implementation, verification and rollback, guided by deny-by-default firewall practice (NIST SP 800-41).

Key findings

  • Device logins used one shared local account, with no central authentication and no audit trail. This was remediated by moving devices to central TACACS+ login, starting with the first router.
  • Documentation said two edge routers had identical access lists. On the devices, one list was truncated and the other was missing.
  • An automatic gateway update converted the firewall to a zone-based model. I reviewed all 139 migrated policies and confirmed none was lost or changed.
  • New custom firewall zones did not allow reply traffic by default, which would have silently dropped server responses. This was caught and handled before cutover.
  • One alert rule caused most of the noise; seven others had never been tested.

Prioritization

I ranked items by risk to the shared home network, effort, and what each one unblocked. Management-plane security came first: highest risk and a prerequisite for later projects. Anything that could affect family connectivity was staged with a one-step rollback.

Done

  • A hardened jump host requiring an SSH key plus a one-time code.
  • A TACACS+ and RADIUS server on a dedicated management VLAN.
  • That VLAN moved into its own firewall zone with explicit, logged policies, prepared while empty and cut over in one step.
  • Central login and command accounting on the first router, with a console-only break-glass account.

Planned

  • Extend central login to the rest of the lab.
  • Further access-control hardening.
  • Alert tuning.

What I learned

Verify against the device and the server's own logs, never against the documentation or a counter that merely looks healthy.
